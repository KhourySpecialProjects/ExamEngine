ExamEngine gives every final exam a day, a time and a room. You upload a few CSV files that
describe the term, choose a few settings, and ExamEngine builds a schedule that keeps students
and instructors out of overlapping exams and fits every exam in a room that is large enough.
Anything it could not avoid, it reports, so you can see exactly where the schedule needs
attention.

New to ExamEngine? Start with the [Quickstart](/docs/start/quickstart): it takes you from a new
account to an exported schedule with a set of sample files.

## Key ideas

**Dataset.** The set of CSV files for one term, uploaded under a name you choose. Three files are
required: courses (one row per course section, or CRN), enrollments (which student is in which
section) and rooms (each room and how many people it seats). Three are optional: room blockouts
(times a room can't be used), combined exams and common exams (see below). You can generate as
many schedules as you like from one dataset.

**Schedule.** The result of one generation run: every exam with its day, time and room, saved with
the settings you chose and the conflicts that remain. Schedules are kept until you delete them, so
you can try different settings and compare the results.

**Exam block.** A day has up to five exam blocks: 8AM-10AM, 10:30AM-12:30PM, 1PM-3PM,
3:30PM-5:30PM and 6PM-8PM. The exam period lasts up to seven days, Monday to Sunday. You choose
how many days and whether to use the evening block when you generate.

**Combined and common exams.** A combined exam is several sections that sit one exam together, at
the same time and in the same room. A common exam is several sections that sit their exams at the
same time, each in its own room.

**Conflict.** A situation the schedule could not avoid. The serious ones (hard conflicts) are a
student or instructor with two exams at the same time, or with more exams in one day than the
daily limit you set. The others are back-to-back exams (exams in consecutive blocks on the same
day) and large courses (100 or more students) scheduled Thursday or later.

**Unscheduled exam.** An exam that got no day, time or room, for example because more students
take it than the largest room seats. ExamEngine lists every unscheduled exam with the reason.

**Classic and Optimized.** The two ways to generate a schedule. Classic is fast and places exams
one at a time. Optimized keeps improving the schedule for a few seconds and usually ends with
fewer conflicts and back-to-back exams.
