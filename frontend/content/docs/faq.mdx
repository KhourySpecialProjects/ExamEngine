## Your data

### What files do I need to upload?

Three CSV files are required: courses, enrollments and rooms. Three more are optional: room
blockouts, combined exams and common exams. Click **Upload CSV** under **Datasets Controls** in
the Controls Center, name the dataset, add the files and click **Upload All Files**. See step 4 of
the [Quickstart](/docs/start/quickstart#4-upload-a-dataset).

### What columns are required in each CSV?

- **Courses**: `CRN`, `CourseID` and the enrollment count (`Enrollment` or `num_students`). The
  instructor column (`Instructor Name` or `instructor_name`) is optional, but a section without an
  instructor is left out of instructor conflicts and limits.
- **Enrollments**: `NUID`, `CRN`.
- **Rooms**: `Room` (or `room_name`) and `Capacity`, plus an optional `LargeOnly` column (yes or
  no). Mark at most one room, and it must seat more than every other room: exams too big for every
  other room can only go there, and no other exam may use it.
- **Room blockouts**: `Room`, `Day`, `Block`. **Combined exams**: `ExamGroup`, `CRN`.
  **Common exams**: `Common_Group`, `CRN`.

Headers are matched without regard to case, and a few other common names are accepted. Extra
columns are ignored. If a file is rejected, see
[Troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting#an-upload-fails).

### Can I reuse datasets across runs?

Yes. A dataset stays in the dataset picker until you delete it, and you can generate any number of
schedules from it without uploading the files again. Each schedule needs a name you haven't used
before.

### How do I remove a dataset?

Select it in the dataset picker under **Datasets Controls**, click the trash icon next to it and
click **Continue**. This can't be undone. Schedules made from the dataset are kept and show it as
**Deleted**; you can still view, export, share, validate and compare them, but Late Add and student
lookups no longer work. See [The dataset was
deleted](/docs/reference/troubleshooting#the-dataset-was-deleted).

## Generating

### How do I adjust constraints?

In the **Generate Schedule** dialog, before you generate:

- **Maximum Exams Per Student Per Day** and **Maximum Exams Per Instructor Per Day** (1 to 5).
- **Maximum Days for Exam Period** (1 to 7, starting Monday).
- **Exam Blocks Per Day**: 5, or 4 to leave out the 6PM-8PM block.
- **Algorithm**: **Classic** or **Optimized**.
- **Optimization Time** (Optimized only): 5, 15 or 30 seconds.
- **Avoid Back-to-Back Exams** and **Prioritize Large Classes**.
- **Promote to Larger Rooms**: after scheduling, moves the fullest exams into larger free rooms in
  the same block, at most twice their enrollment, so instructors can use alternate-seat spacing.

See [Settings](/docs/generating/settings) for what each one does.

### How are conflicts handled?

Both engines, Classic and Optimized, only put an exam in a room that is big enough, free and not
blocked out, and keep combined and common exams together. They try hardest to keep students and
instructors from having two exams at once or more exams in a day than your limits allow, then try
to avoid back-to-back exams and to schedule courses of 100 or more students before Thursday.

Conflicts are kept as low as possible, not forbidden: if some can't be avoided, the schedule is
still made and reports them. Two settings apply to one engine only:

- **Avoid Back-to-Back Exams** affects Optimized only. Classic always tries to avoid back-to-back
  exams.
- **Prioritize Large Classes** affects Classic only, which then places the largest exams first.
  Optimized ignores it.

See [Classic vs Optimized](/docs/generating/classic-vs-optimized) and
[Conflict types](/docs/reference/conflict-types).

### How long does generation take?

Classic places all exams in a single pass. Optimized keeps improving the schedule for up to the
**Optimization Time** you choose (5, 15 or 30 seconds), then saves it. You can generate again with
other settings at any time without uploading your data again.

### Do I need to restart after changing parameters?

No. Choose new settings and generate again under a new name. Each run is saved as its own
schedule, so you can compare them.

### Why might a run fail?

The most common cause is a schedule name you have already used, including the name of an earlier
run that failed. Others are rows in the courses file without a CRN, course code or enrollment.
Missing columns and malformed files are caught earlier, when you upload. Rooms that are too small
don't make a run fail: those exams are left unscheduled. See
[Troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting#generation-fails).

## Reviewing

### What if conflicts remain?

The **Conflicts** tab lists every conflict by person or by course, and **Statistics** shows the
totals and what needs attention. Exams that couldn't be scheduled are listed in **Statistics** with
the reason, and marked **Unscheduled** in the **List** tab. Change your settings or your files and
generate again. Courses with no enrolled students are skipped, since nobody sits their exam.

### How do I interpret the conflict breakdown?

The **Conflicts** tab has one tab per conflict type, showing the students, instructors or courses
involved; **Conflict Definitions** at the bottom explains each type. **Statistics** shows the
totals. Use them to decide which settings or input files to change before you generate again. See
[Conflict types](/docs/reference/conflict-types).

### Where do I see what changed?

Each schedule shows the settings it was generated with at the top of its page. To see how
schedules differ, tick two to four of them in the schedules list and click **Compare**: it shows
which settings differ, and their conflicts, calendar shape and room use side by side. It doesn't
list individual exams that moved. See [Comparing schedules](/docs/reviewing/comparing).

### How do I compare schedules?

In the schedules list, tick the boxes of two to four completed schedules, yours or shared with you,
and click **Compare** in the bar that appears. The first column is the **Baseline**; every other
schedule's numbers show whether they are better or worse than it. Use a column's menu to **Set as
baseline**, move or remove it, or **Add schedule** to add another. See
[Comparing schedules](/docs/reviewing/comparing).

### How do I check that a schedule is correct?

Open the schedule and click **Validate Schedule**, then **Run Validation**. It re-checks the
schedule against the dataset's original files without changing anything: every course accounted
for, rooms big enough, free and not blocked out, combined and common exams together, the conflict
counts, and the data itself. Each check passes, warns, fails or is skipped, with examples. Anyone
who can view the schedule can run it. See [Validating a schedule](/docs/reviewing/validating).

### How do I see one student's or instructor's exams?

Next to a student NUId or instructor ID (in the Conflicts tab, a course's conflict details, the List
view's Instructor column, the late additions table and Late Add results), the calendar icon opens
that person's exams in the schedule: a short list, then the exam week with each exam's CRN, course
and room. Two different exams in the same block show in red; sections of one combined or common
exam count as one exam. Opened from Late Add results, the exam being added is shown dashed in the
block you're looking at. **Open in Explore** shows the same person in the Explore tab. Anyone who
can view the schedule can use it.

### What does the Explore tab show?

Explore looks up one room, student, instructor or course in the schedule and shows its exams as a
calendar (the default) or a list. Pick a room from the list of every room to also see its blocked
times: striped orange on the calendar, and under **Blocked times** below the list. An exam in a
blocked slot shows in red. Sections of a combined exam in the same block are drawn as one framed
block with the group's name, room and total students; a common exam has a violet badge that lists
all its sections and their rooms. The list's Group column names both. Type a student's NUId, or
choose an instructor; recent lookups are listed for quick switching. Choose a CRN to see when its
exam is, or a course code to see all its sections. Rooms in the results, and instructors in the
list, are links to their own lookup. The room or course is kept in the page address so you can
share the link; student and instructor IDs never are (they stay in this browser tab until you close
it). Anyone who can view the schedule can use Explore. See [Explore](/docs/reviewing/explore).

### Can I export results?

Yes. **Export Schedule** on the schedule page downloads every exam as one CSV file, including
unscheduled ones: CRN, course, day, start and end time, room, capacity, size, valid (false when the
exam has a conflict or is over its room's capacity), and instructor. Anyone who can view the
schedule can export it. See
[Exporting](/docs/reviewing/exporting).

## Changing and sharing

### Can I add an exam that missed generation?

Yes. The schedule's owner can open it and use **Late Add**. Enter the CRN, course code and
instructor ID, then **Find blocks**. The CRN must be in the enrollments file but not already in the
schedule. No scheduled exam moves: the exam only goes in rooms that are free and not blocked out.
When some blocks have no student or instructor conflict, only those blocks are listed. If there are
none, every block with a free room is listed, fewest conflicts first, and each conflict expands to
the students or instructor involved. Searching never changes the schedule. Late Add is unavailable
when the schedule's dataset was deleted. See [Late Add](/docs/changing/late-add).

### How do I save a late add, and what happens to the original?

Pick a block (and room), name the new schedule, and choose **Save as new schedule**. The schedule
you started from is never changed: the save creates a new version with all of its exams plus the
late one, and opens it. A block with a student or instructor double-book or per-day limit conflict
can only be saved after you tick the box that lists them; back-to-back and large-course conflicts
don't need it. Versions chain, so a late add on that new version makes a third one with both late
exams. The schedule page shows which schedule a version is a late add to, the original it started
from, and a table of every late-added exam; those exams are marked **Late add** in the List and
calendar views, and the schedules list shows **Late add** in the Algorithm column. If the schedule
already has a newer version, Late Add warns you and links to it: a new version made from the older
one won't include the newer one's late adds.

### Can I share a schedule with teammates?

Yes. The schedule's owner can open it, click **Share**, choose a user and click **Share
Schedule**. Shared users can view the schedule and use every tab, **Validate Schedule**, **Export
Schedule** and Compare, but can't share it further or use Late Add. To stop sharing, click the red
**×** next to the user under **Current Shares**. See [Sharing](/docs/working-with-others/sharing).
